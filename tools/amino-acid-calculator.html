What do these percentages mean?

100% means the food serving is estimated to supply the WHO adult average requirement for that essential amino acid or paired group, using your body weight. It is not a recommended upper limit, proof of adequate total nutrition, or a claim about muscle protein synthesis. Percentages may exceed 100%.

Methionine + cysteine and phenylalanine + tyrosine are paired because requirements are set for the combined groups.

How should I interpret the targets?

The 0.8 g/kg/day reference is the general adult protein RDA, not a muscle-building prescription. The ISSN cites 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day for many exercising adults. PROT-AGE recommends 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day for healthy older adults and at least 1.2 g/kg/day for active older adults. The ~2.5–2.8 g leucine/meal guide comes from older-adult expert guidance; it is not a strict threshold. Age alone does not determine a person's needs.

Protein quantity, amino acid quality, total daily intake, training, and recovery all matter. This tool does not assess digestibility, absorption, total diets, or actual muscle protein synthesis. Ask a qualified clinician for individual guidance, particularly with kidney disease or medically prescribed protein restrictions.

Data quality & interpretation

Estimated composition, not laboratory-verified food values. This prototype includes a 232-food catalogue with approximate protein values and generalized amino acid patterns by food family. It is useful for exploring relative differences, but should not be used for clinical decisions or precise nutrition logging. Cup-to-gram equivalents are food-specific approximations; mL for non-liquids is a volume proxy, not a measured density.

For a production-grade database, replace each estimate with measured per-100-g amino acid records and source IDs from USDA FoodData Central. Reference: WHO/FAO/UNU protein and amino acid requirements, Table 23. Adult average requirements are different from individualized clinical or athletic targets. Protein from all foods over the full day matters.
